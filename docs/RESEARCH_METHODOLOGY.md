# Research Methodology for RDOS

## Scientific objective

Optimize the quality of *verified understanding per constrained research budget*, not hypothesis count or persuasive language. Research taste is treated as a decision process whose quality must be independently evaluated.

## Research funnel

1. Frame a question and resources.
2. Retrieve/verify closest primary literature, including alternative names and approaches.
3. Construct an assumption/contradiction/failure map.
4. Generate mechanistically diverse hypotheses with predictions and falsifiers.
5. Challenge novelty, causality, experiment feasibility and scientific significance.
6. Preregister a discriminating experiment with controlled baseline and metrics.
7. Execute in a bounded approved environment; archive raw artifacts.
8. Analyze outcomes against predictions, update evidence, preserve failures.
9. Review decisions and plan the next information-rich action.

## Distinguish research statements

- **Observation**: what the instrument, dataset or executable produced.
- **Source claim**: what an identified paper reports under its stated conditions.
- **Interpretation**: what the evidence may imply, acknowledging confounders.
- **Hypothesis**: a falsifiable candidate explanation with competing alternatives.
- **Scientific conclusion**: a scoped claim supported by inspected evidence.

Absence of a paper from search results is not evidence of novelty. Matching historical publications is not equivalent to scientific value. Negative results may improve knowledge even when performance does not improve.

## Research-taste objective

Benchmark whether better research decisions identify hidden mechanisms or robust findings using fewer verified resources. Compare strategies under equal access and budget; disclose leakage, model knowledge, evaluator subjectivity and uncertainty. Do not assert that self-critique is independent review.
